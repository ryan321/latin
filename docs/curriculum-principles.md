# Curriculum principles (all years)

Standing rules for authoring lessons. Apply to Year 1 and every later year.

## English grammar before Latin forms

**Whenever we introduce a new *type* of grammar concept, teach it first in English (or plain language the student already has)—with enough practice to grasp it—before teaching the Latin morphology or syntax that uses it.**

Do **not** assume the student already understands the concept at the depth Latin will demand.

### What this means

| Do | Don’t |
|----|--------|
| Name the job in English with many examples | Open with a Latin chart and hope endings teach the idea |
| Drill the concept on English sentences until the standard is met | One sentence of English, then a full Latin paradigm |
| Only then map the concept onto Latin forms | Use Latin terms (*imperfect*, *ablative of means*) before the idea is solid |
| Re-check the concept lightly when Latin gets harder | Assume Unit 1 memory lasts forever without review |

### Good precedents already in the course

- **Parts of speech** (Unit 1) before declining/conjugating  
- **Case meanings** (Unit 2) before first-declension charts  

### Required concept bridges (Year 1 pattern)

Before the Latin that depends on them, ship dedicated **concept** lessons (often a short unit) for:

| Concept (English / plain language) | Before Latin unit on… |
|------------------------------------|------------------------|
| Person & number; subject–verb | Personal endings / conjugations |
| Tense & aspect meanings (present, past ongoing, simple past, future, completed, had done, will have done) | Imperfect, future, perfect system |
| Agreement (words matching number/gender/case jobs) | Adjective agreement |
| Relative clauses & antecedents (*who/which/that*) | *quī, quae, quod* |
| Active vs passive voice; agent (*by…*) | Passive endings |
| Comparative / superlative meaning | Latin comparison |
| Participles in English (*-ing*, *-ed*) | Latin participles |

Later years: same rule for mood (subjunctive), conditionals, indirect statement, sequence of tenses, etc.—**concept unit → Latin forms → reading**.

### How many lessons?

Enough for the student to **use the idea correctly on English** before Latin:

- Simple idea (e.g. “plural means more than one”): 1 focused lesson may be enough if Unit 1 already drilled it.  
- Heavy idea (tense system, voice, relative clauses): **several lessons + checkpoint**, not a single paragraph in a Latin teach screen.

If the Latin unit is still failing because students confuse *meanings*, add more English concept practice—not only more Latin drills.

### Lesson design checklist (authors)

Before writing a Latin forms lesson, ask:

1. **What concept** does this Latin encode?  
2. **Where was that concept taught** in English, with practice and a standard?  
3. If nowhere → **author the concept lessons first** (or block the Latin unit until they exist).  
4. Does the Latin teach open by **linking** back to the English concept in one clear sentence?  
5. Are seed activities testing **meaning** as well as forms?

### Relationship to mastery

Concept lessons have their own **standards** (English analysis, matching, short answers, multi-select). Meeting them unlocks (or prepares for) the Latin unit. AI remediation may re-teach the English concept when Latin errors show conceptual gaps—not only wrong endings.

## Grammar-first, still original

Henle-inspired sequence and rigor; **original** explanations and exercises. Classical pronunciation. Culture in every unit where natural.

## Cumulative review

New Latin reuses old concepts. Checkpoints mix **concept labels** and **Latin forms** so the student keeps both layers.

## Reading passages (continuous Latin)

Isolated drills are not enough. After each major grammar block, include **short connected passages** with comprehension questions (see `docs/reading-passages.md`).

- Difficulty matches **what has been taught** (English concepts + Latin forms so far).
- Students practice **reading for meaning**, then grammar-in-context.
- Frequency increases toward Year 1 reading unit and capstone; later years add longer authentic-style passages.

## Vocabulary strand

Students cannot live on grammar alone.

- Ship **periodic vocabulary lessons** (batches of ~10–12 high-frequency words) with **drills and quizzes**.  
- **Recycle** those words in reading passages and later grammar practice.  
- Cumulative **vocab quizzes** at mid-arc points and capstone.  

Details: `docs/vocabulary.md` and `content/vocab/year1-batches.json`.
