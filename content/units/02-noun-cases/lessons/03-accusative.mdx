## Accusative — the direct object

The <KeyTerm>accusative</KeyTerm> case marks the **direct object**: who or what **receives the action** of the verb.

### English sense

- The girl sees **the land**.  
- They carry **water**.  
- He loves **Rome**.  

Ask: *Sees what? Carries what?* → direct object → accusative in Latin.

### Subject vs object

| English | Job | Latin case (idea) |
|---------|-----|-------------------|
| The **girl** sees the land | subject | nominative |
| The girl sees the **land** | direct object | accusative |

### Mini Latin

<LatinExample
  latin="Puella terram spectat."
  english="The girl looks at / watches the land."
  note="Puella = nominative subject; terram = accusative object; spectat = verb."
/>

Word order can vary in Latin; endings keep the jobs clear.

<Callout kind="warning">
  Do not assume “first noun = subject.” Always check the case idea (and later the ending).
</Callout>
