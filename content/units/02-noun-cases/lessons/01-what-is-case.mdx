## What is case?

<KeyTerm>Case</KeyTerm> is the **form of a noun** that shows its **job** in the sentence: who is doing the action, who is receiving it, possession, location, and more.

### English vs Latin

English mostly uses **word order**:

- The **girl** sees the **land**.  
- The **land** sees the **girl**.  

Same words; different order → different meaning.

Latin can shuffle order more freely because **endings** mark the jobs:

- Subject form of “girl” vs object form of “land” stay clear even if words move.

### The five cases you need first

We will learn these one by one (and use them constantly):

| Case | Core job (starter sense) |
|------|---------------------------|
| **Nominative** | Subject (who/what is doing or being) |
| **Accusative** | Direct object (who/what is acted on) |
| **Genitive** | Of / possession |
| **Dative** | To / for (indirect object) |
| **Ablative** | By / with / from; place where with *in* |

(There is also a **vocative** for direct address — light touch later.)
