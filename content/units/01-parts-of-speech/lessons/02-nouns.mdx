## Nouns

A <KeyTerm>noun</KeyTerm> names a **person**, **place**, **thing**, or **idea**.

### Examples (English)

| Kind | Examples |
|------|----------|
| Person | girl, farmer, Caesar |
| Place | Rome, forest, road |
| Thing | water, table, gift |
| Idea | peace, courage, love |

### In Latin (preview)

You will meet these early:

- <Latin>puella</Latin> — girl  
- <Latin>aqua</Latin> — water  
- <Latin>terra</Latin> — land, earth  
- <Latin>via</Latin> — road, way  
- <Latin>Rōma</Latin> — Rome  

### Gender (light preview)

Latin nouns have **gender**: masculine, feminine, or neuter.  
Many 1st-declension nouns (Unit 3) are **feminine** — but not always (*poēta*, “poet,” is masculine). For now, just know gender exists; we will attach it to dictionary forms later.

### What nouns will do next

In the next unit, nouns change **case** (nominative, accusative, …) to show their job in the sentence. English mostly uses word order for that; Latin uses endings.
