## Adjectives and other parts of speech

### Adjectives

An <KeyTerm>adjective</KeyTerm> **describes** a noun: size, color, quality, number sense, and more.

- the **large** villa  
- **good** water  
- a **Roman** road  

In Latin, adjectives will **agree** with their nouns (gender, number, case). That comes after you can decline nouns.

### The supporting cast

| Part of speech | Job | English examples |
|----------------|-----|------------------|
| **Adverb** | Describes a verb (how/when/where) | quickly, often, not |
| **Preposition** | Relates a noun to the rest of the sentence | in, with, from, to, into |
| **Conjunction** | Joins words or clauses | and, but, or |
| **Pronoun** | Stands in for a noun | I, you, he, she, they, who |

### Prepositions matter early

Latin *in* + ablative will mean “in/on” (place where). English *in the forest* → Latin will look like *in silvā* once you have ablative.

  You do not need to memorize every label today. You <strong>do</strong> need noun / verb / adjective cold — and a working sense of preposition.
</Callout>
